What this is

The Argentina Economic Monitor collects the main statistics on Argentina's economy in one place: activity and GDP, international trade, prices, money and the central bank, the exchange rate, banks, and the public accounts and debt. Every chart can be explored, compared across presidencies, and downloaded as an image or as the underlying data.

The data are fetched automatically twice a day from each source, checked before they are accepted, and stored with their full revision history, so any chart can be rebuilt as it looked on an earlier date. The site has no editorial line on its charts: they show what the sources publish and how we transform it. Interpretation belongs elsewhere, in Economic Order.

Who is behind it

The Monitor is a project of Economic Order, by Nicolás Cachanosky. The code, the data and every past vintage are public in the project repository.

Sources

Most series are official statistics: INDEC, the BCRA, the Ministry of Economy's Finance and Treasury Secretariats, and, for comparison, the US Treasury. Where no official series exists, or where an official one was compromised (consumer prices in 2007–2015), the site uses the best available alternative and says so on the chart. Market prices that no official body publishes (the informal and bond-market dollars, dollar futures) come from market-data services and are marked as unofficial wherever they appear. The Methodology page documents every source and calculation.

How to cite

Please cite the original source of each series (shown under every chart) and the Monitor for the transformation or chart:

Cachanosky, Nicolás. Argentina Economic Monitor. Economic Order. https://ncachanosky.github.io/argentina-economic-monitor/ (accessed date).

Each chart has its own address, the page's address plus the chart's anchor (for example, …/exchange-rate/#ind-fx_official), and links to pages that have been renamed keep working.

Corrections and contact

If a number looks wrong, a source has changed, or a chart is unclear, please open an issue on GitHub; corrections are logged there in the open. For anything else, write through Economic Order.
